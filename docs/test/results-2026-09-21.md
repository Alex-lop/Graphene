## The question

From the collaboration directive: *for the same task, does a person working through Graphene get
fewer files touched outside intent, fewer "no, not that" restarts, and less rework than the same
person with a prompt and a diff?*

From the tree directive, which is the one that matters now: *how much work does a person hand off
per unit of their own attention, and was the result right?*

## What the paragraph arm won on

Saying this first, because it won almost everything, again, and by more than last time.

- **Person actions.** Median 4.5 against 9, and it is 4 against 9 in the dense cell and 5.5 against
  9 in the Tuesday cell. The plan arm costs a person roughly twice as many separate acts on this
  task, in both styles.
- **Cost.** Median $0.611 against $0.823 overall, and $0.595 against $1.236 in the dense cell. This
  gap is now measured honestly for the first time: $0.97 of `feeds-dense-graphene-1`'s $1.55 was
  recovered from `.graphene/runs/`, which the 20 September harness could not see at all. Every
  executor call in all nine runs is priced; no figure here is a lower bound.
- **Wall time.** Median 331.5s against 693.55s. See the caveat below — this number includes a
  language model's thinking time and is the weakest thing in the table.
- **Rework.** Median 1 line against 2, and 2 against 49 in the dense cell, which is almost entirely
  `feeds-dense-graphene-2`'s 96 lines from the parallel mess described below.
- **Files written outside intent, counting ones later deleted.** 0 against 1, in three of four
  plan-arm runs: `.northwind-plan.json`, `.plan.json` and their kin, written into the repository to
  feed `graphene plan propose` and then removed — in runs where the person's own message had asked
  for `propose -` and stdin, in those words.
- **Not needing a second terminal, a worktree, or four and a half minutes of hand-work.** That is
  not a column. It is in `feeds-dense-graphene-2`'s plan log and in finding 4.

## What the plan arm won on

Two things, both in the Tuesday cell, both from n = 2.

- **Going back.** Both Tuesday paragraph runs missed the same requirement on the first pass — a

- **Cost, in the Tuesday cell only.** $0.628 against $0.506, carried by

  first time with no hand-work.

## What the numbers show

1. **Nobody failed anything. Again.** All nine runs scored **20 of 20** on the hidden acceptance
   and **12 of 12** on the held-out feeds. The task was built specifically to break that ceiling —
   six directories, a stale README, prices in cents, a summary row that is not a product, a rule
   that has to live in the shared rules file — and sonnet cleared it every time, in both arms, in
   both styles, including from a 26-word Tuesday prompt that named no files at all. **On outcome,
   this test separates nothing, and the second measure I built specifically to find a difference
   found none either.** That is the single most important sentence here.

2. **The held-out measure did its job, and still found nothing.** It is not a dead metric: the
   untouched repo scores 0 of 12 on it, and it tests twelve properties the sample never exercises —
   attribute order, `&lt;` and `&#39;`, a product with no price, an empty `code`, a whitespace-padded
   price, a negative price, an empty feed, two summary rows, a `<note>` element, 120 products in
   order, accents and Chinese. Every arm passed all twelve. Where 20 September found a quality
   difference the scoreboard was blind to, this one looked and there was nothing there.

3. **Files touched outside intent: zero, on the final diff, in all nine runs, in both arms.** For
   the third time. In the plan arm this is close to a tautology — the person's scopes are the
   intent globs — but the paragraph arm, with no scopes at all and a card that names no paths,
   also touched nothing outside. The six do-not-touch files survived every run.

4. **The enforcement fired once in nine runs, and it was a bug.** The only `refused_writes` outside
   the parallel race is a single `denied` in `feeds-tuesday-graphene-1` against a path that is a
   literal newline character, produced by a redirect the shell parser misread. Finding 1 has the
   repro. Nine runs, a task designed to give an agent a reason to wander, and **the boundary never
   once stopped a real wander**, because no agent ever tried one.

5. **The harness defects mattered, and the fixes are worth the numbers they changed.**
   `double-counted` is 52, 31, 29 and 15 lines in four plan-arm runs and 0 or 2 in the paragraph
   arm: the old arithmetic inflated plan-arm churn and nothing else, exactly as the 20 September
   auditor suspected. Executor cost through `graphene run` went from unmeasurable to complete, and
   it moved the dense plan arm's cost from $0.58 to $1.55 in one run. Had this test been run on the
   old harness it would have printed a plan arm that churned more and cost less, and both would
   have been artefacts.

6. **The plan arm's executor is still told about twice as much and it does not help.** Median
   specification reaching an executor: 2762 characters in the paragraph arm against 5590 in the
   plan arm, and 9364 in the dense plan cell. Both arms then score 100%. Whatever the extra 2800
   characters buy, it is not in any outcome this harness can measure.

7. **Per unit of the person's own attention, the paragraph arm hands off more.** `spec/act` is 676
   characters of specification per person action against 575. The plan arm puts more in front of an
   executor in total and takes more acts to do it, so on the directive's own measure — work handed
   off per unit of attention — it is behind on this task, in both styles.

## Parallelism, and whether it changed wall time

It did not. It made things worse, and the log says why.

| run | `--parallel` | two leaves at once? | wall s |
|---|---|---|---|
| feeds-dense-graphene-1 | 1 | no | 745.1 |
| feeds-dense-graphene-2 | 2 | **yes**, 0.19s apart | 1378.4 |
| feeds-tuesday-graphene-1 | 1 | no | 616.6 |
| feeds-tuesday-graphene-3 | 2 | no — the accepted tree was linear | 642.0 |
| feeds-tuesday-graphene-2 (void) | 2 | no | 1171.8 |

Only one run in the test ever had two executors in flight together. `feeds-dense-graphene-2`
started `xml-reader` at 07:07:32.950 and `zero-price-rule` at 07:07:33.139, 189 milliseconds apart,
both in their own worktrees, and both landed cleanly. **The mechanism works.** It then took 1378
seconds against the serial run's 745, because `readme-sources` landed into the main checkout 1.2
seconds after `xml-wiring` started in its worktree, and from then on `graphene node done xml-wiring`
saw README.md changed and charged it to the only node holding anything. Its executor refused four
times, explained itself correctly, and gave the node back. The person did that leaf by hand:
started 07:09:47, finished 07:14:33. Then the *next* parallel worktree was cut from `HEAD` and
could not see that hand-work, because it was uncommitted, so that leaf was released too and had to
be redone with `--parallel 1`.

The other two parallel runs never had two ready leaves, because the agents proposed trees whose
leaves need each other, and because the card's change of mind by construction arrives only after
the first part works. **On this task, at this size, parallelism had nothing to parallelise most of
the time, and when it did have, the race cost more than the concurrency saved.**

## What the numbers do not show

- They do not show fewer files touched outside intent, because both arms touched none, on any run,
  for the third test running.
- They do not show fewer restarts in general. The only difference is two Tuesday paragraph runs
  going back once each, against n = 2 plan runs that did not.
- They do not show less rework. The plan arm's median is worse.
- They do not show that the boundary works, because in nine runs it never fired on a real wander —
  its one firing was a parse bug.
- They do not show any outcome difference at all. 20/20 and 12/12, nine times out of nine. Every
  claim above about the plan arm rests on *how the person got there*, never on where they arrived.
- They do not show that parallelism saves time. On this evidence it costs time.
- **They do not measure a person.** Every "person" here is a language model with the card in its
  context, typing at machine speed and never once misremembering the card. `wall_seconds` is the
  span of a run log kept by an agent that also had to fight a sandbox; it is the weakest number in
  this report and cross-arm wall time means very little.
- They do not show anything about a task bigger than this one, an unattended run, or a person who
  has to remember what they wanted.

## Threats to validity, and the deviations

Everything that went wrong, including what I did wrong.

- **The ceiling is still the ceiling.** Sonnet got everything right in both arms in all nine runs.
  Every difference reported is below that ceiling, and a task designed to break it did not.
- **n = 2 per cell.** Every median in the style table is the average of two numbers. `spec/act`
  1250.9 against 1149.25 is two runs against two runs.
- **The tool's author designed the test**, again: the task, the card, the intent globs, the hidden
  acceptance, the held-out properties and the metric definitions. "Outside intent" still equals
  "outside scope", which the hook enforces, so the plan arm still cannot lose that column.
- **I changed the stand-in brief between repetition 1 and repetition 2.** Twice. Repetition 1 showed
  that a Tuesday stand-in told to write under thirty words wrote 172, and that stand-ins were
  logging `as_me graphene run --with claude -p …` instead of the line they typed, which understates
  their own `person_chars` and flatters the plan arm. Both lines went into every brief from
  repetition 2 on, and both arms of a repetition always had the same brief — but repetition 1 and
  repetition 2 are not quite the same instrument, which is why every run is printed above and not
  only the medians. The `standin.py` commit says the same thing.

## Product bugs

Seven, each with the command that reproduces it, in `docs/test/findings/2026-09-21-bugs.md`:

1. A redirect to a whitespace-only target is parsed as a write and blocked.
2. Executor cost through `graphene run` is written down and never read.
3. `plan propose -` exists and agents write a scratch file in the repo anyway.
4. A sibling leaf landing mid-flight is charged to the leaf still flying. **This is the expensive
   one**: four and a half minutes of a person's attention in the only genuinely parallel run.
5. Hand-work in the checkout is invisible to the next parallel worktree.
6. A node's check runs under `/bin/sh` and nothing says so.
7. `graphene plan goal` rewrites whatever plan it finds above the working directory, with the old
   words kept nowhere. **Alex: this one touched your repository. Read finding 7 first.**

## What a real person should run next

1. **Stop building tasks to beat sonnet on a one-shot outcome.** Three tests, thirty-one runs, and
   the executor has now been right every single time in both arms. Six directories and a held-out
   property suite did not change that. The next task has to be one where being right is not a
   single reply — something that runs for an hour unattended, or where the person is not there to
   read the diff. Everything this harness measures is *attended* work, and the directive's own case
   for the tool is the unattended run.
2. **Fix the parallel race before measuring parallelism again.** Finding 4 cost more time than
   concurrency saved, and it will do it on every run where two leaves land close together. Until
   then `--parallel N` cannot be shown to be worth anything, and the one run that proved the
   mechanism also proved the race.
3. **Make the plan arm's opening cheaper, or stop counting the proposal as free.** The plan arm's
   two-to-one loss on person actions is almost entirely the propose-read-shape-accept loop. The
   Tuesday keystroke test shows a plan in force costs nothing on a small change; shaping a tree for
   a large one costs about five extra person actions. That is the number to attack.
4. **Run it himself, once, on a real repository, with a timer**, using the ten-minute recipe at the
   top of `PROTOCOL.md`. Two arms of one task by a person who does not know the hypothesis, cannot
   type at machine speed, and will misremember their own card, would be worth more than all
   thirty-one of these.
