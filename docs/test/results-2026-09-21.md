# The second collaboration test: results, 21 September 2026

Nine runs of one new task — two arms, two person styles, two repetitions, one of them void and
re-run — plus four runs of a separate keystroke comparison. A different stand-in played the person
in every run, none of them was told what was being measured, and no two executors were ever in
flight at the same time.

The `graphene` under test is a wheel built from **563c1f62151e9f4bdaf004f550cc6ff3b90a92f0**, the
tip of `tree` when this started, installed into a venv whose `bin` was first on `PATH` for every

one, and losing it is what voided it.

## The question

From the collaboration directive: *for the same task, does a person working through Graphene get
fewer files touched outside intent, fewer "no, not that" restarts, and less rework than the same
person with a prompt and a diff?*

From the tree directive, which is the one that matters now: *how much work does a person hand off
per unit of their own attention, and was the result right?*

## What the paragraph arm won on

Saying this first, because it won almost everything, again, and by more than last time.

- **Person actions.** Median 4.5 against 9, and it is 4 against 9 in the dense cell and 5.5 against
  9 in the Tuesday cell. The plan arm costs a person roughly twice as many separate acts on this
  task, in both styles.
- **Cost.** Median **$0.611 against $1.150**, a 1.88× gap. The printed table says $0.823, and it is
  wrong: three of the four valid plan-arm runs never logged a `result` for the executor call that
  proposed their tree, although the brief told them to. Those calls are still on disk as
  `--output-format json` in each run's own `tmp/`, and adding them back gives $1.2407, $1.0597 and
  $0.7027 where the table shows $0.921, $0.726 and $0.287. The corrected cell medians are $0.595
  against $1.395 (dense) and $0.628 against $0.881 (Tuesday). The adversarial auditor found this;

  and $1.07. `tally.py` cannot see it — `executor_calls_unpriced` only counts logged results with
  no cost and non-JSON files under `.graphene/runs/` — so its `0` is a false assurance and should
  be read as "nothing I was given was unpriced", not "nothing is missing".
- **Wall time.** Median 331.5s against 693.55s. See the caveat below — this number includes a

- **Rework.** Median 1 line against 2 — but do not use this. Both Tuesday paragraph runs have

## What the numbers show

1. **Nobody failed anything. Again.** All nine runs scored **20 of 20** on the hidden acceptance
   and **12 of 12** on the held-out feeds. The task was built specifically to break that ceiling —
   six directories, a stale README, prices in cents, a summary row that is not a product, a rule
   that has to live in the shared rules file — and sonnet cleared it every time, in both arms, in
   both styles, including from a 26-word Tuesday prompt that named no files at all. **On outcome,
   this test separates nothing, and the second measure I built specifically to find a difference
   found none either.** That is the single most important sentence here.

2. **The held-out measure found nothing, and it is weaker than I claimed.** It tests twelve
   properties the sample never exercises — attribute order, `&lt;` and `&#39;`, a product with no
   price, an empty `code`, a whitespace-padded price, a negative price, an empty feed, two summary
   rows, a `<note>` element, 120 products in order, accents and Chinese — and every arm passed all
   twelve. But the auditor built a deliberately lazy reader and a regex fitted to the sample's
   literal product line, and **both score 11 of 12**. Eleven of the twelve properties come free
   from any `ElementTree` call plus the repository's own `normalize()`, `to_cents(…, "minor")` and
   negative-price rule, none of which any executor wrote. So this suite separates lazy from careful
   by one check in twelve, and "12/12 in both arms" is close to saturated. Where 20 September found
   a quality difference the scoreboard was blind to, this one looked with a blunter instrument than
   I thought I had built and saw nothing.

3. **Files touched outside intent: zero, on the final diff, in all nine runs, in both arms.** For
   the third time. In the plan arm this is close to a tautology — the person's scopes are the
   intent globs — but the paragraph arm, with no scopes at all and a card that names no paths,
   also touched nothing outside. The six do-not-touch files survived every run.

4. **The enforcement fired once in nine runs, and it was a bug.** The only `refused_writes` outside
   the parallel race is a single `denied` in `feeds-tuesday-graphene-1` against a path that is a
   literal newline character, produced by a redirect the shell parser misread. Finding 1 has the
   repro. Nine runs, a task designed to give an agent a reason to wander, and **the boundary never
   once stopped a real wander**, because no agent ever tried one.

5. **The harness defects mattered, and the fixes are worth the numbers they changed.**
   `double-counted` is 52, 31, 29 and 15 lines in four plan-arm runs against 2 and 0 and 0 in the
   paragraph arm: the old arithmetic inflated plan-arm churn far more than anyone else's, which is
   what the 20 September auditor suspected — though not *only* the plan arm's, as I first wrote. Executor cost through `graphene run` went from unmeasurable to complete, and
   it moved the dense plan arm's cost from $0.58 to $1.55 in one run. Had this test been run on the
   old harness it would have printed a plan arm that churned more and cost less, and both would
   have been artefacts.

6. **The plan arm's executor is told about twice as much, most of it written by an agent.** Median
   specification reaching an executor: 2762 characters in the paragraph arm against 5590 in the
   plan arm. But `spec_chars` adds every surviving node's title, goal, check and scope to the
   person's own messages, and the auditor split them: 69% to 73% of the plan arm's total is node
   text, in runs whose stand-ins accepted the proposed tree **with no edits at all**. So the
   executor is largely being handed back its own proposal, and calling that "the person's
   specification" overstates what the person did. Both arms then score 100% either way.

7. **Per unit of attention, I cannot say which arm hands off more, and I said I could.** I printed
   `spec/act` at 676 characters per person action for the paragraph arm against 575 for the plan
   arm and concluded the paragraph arm was ahead on the directive's own measure. That number is an
   artefact of the brief. The plan arm's brief prescribes eight numbered steps, each logged as its
   own person action; the paragraph arm's prescribes four, and it fuses "write the message" and
   "launch the executor" into one logged `prompt` while the plan arm pays separate actions for
   `plan accept` and for each `graphene run`. Drop `accept` and `run` and the action medians are
   4.5 against 6.0, not 4.5 against 9 — and `spec/act` **reverses**, to 676 against 911 in the plan
   arm's favour. The person-action gap is real in the sense that those are things a person does;
   it is not a clean measurement, because I decided what counted as an act when I wrote the briefs.
   **The directive's headline measure is the one number in this report I got wrong in my own
   tool's disfavour, and it is still not trustworthy in either direction.**

It did not. It made things worse, and the log says why.

Only one run in the test ever had two executors in flight together. `feeds-dense-graphene-2`
started `xml-reader` at 07:07:32.950 and `zero-price-rule` at 07:07:33.139, 189 milliseconds apart,
both in their own worktrees, and both landed cleanly; it had a second overlapping pair too
(`readme-sources` started 07:07:49.023 while `xml-reader` ran to 07:07:53.224). **The mechanism
works.** It then took 1378
seconds against the serial run's 745, because `readme-sources` landed into the main checkout 1.2
seconds after `xml-wiring` started in its worktree, and from then on `graphene node done xml-wiring`
saw README.md changed and charged it to the only node holding anything. Its executor refused four
times, explained itself correctly, and gave the node back. The person did that leaf by hand:
started 07:09:47, finished 07:14:33 — 4 minutes 45 seconds, which I called "four and a half
minutes of a person's attention" and charged entirely to the race. The auditor checked: the node's
own check **passed 23 seconds in**, at 07:10:10.351, and the rest went on the stand-in editing the
abandoned `.graphene/worktrees/xml-wiring/` copy instead of the outer checkout — their notes say
*"I got this wrong once … That cost real time and attention."* The race caused the hand-work; it
did not cost four and a half minutes of it. That a released node leaves a stale worktree sitting
there for a person to edit by mistake is an eighth sharp edge, and it is not in the findings file.
Then the *next* parallel worktree was cut from `HEAD` and
could not see that hand-work, because it was uncommitted, so that leaf was released too and had to
be redone with `--parallel 1`.

The other two parallel runs never had two ready leaves, because the agents proposed trees whose
leaves need each other, and because the card's change of mind by construction arrives only after
the first part works. **On this task, at this size, parallelism had nothing to parallelise most of
the time, and when it did have, the race cost more than the concurrency saved.**

## What the numbers do not show

- They do not show fewer files touched outside intent, because both arms touched none, on any run,
  for the third test running.
- They do not show fewer restarts in general. The only difference is two Tuesday paragraph runs
  going back once each, against n = 2 plan runs that did not.
- They do not show less rework. The plan arm's median is worse.
- They do not show that the boundary works, because in nine runs it never fired on a real wander —
  its one firing was a parse bug.
- They do not show any outcome difference at all. 20/20 and 12/12, nine times out of nine. Every
  claim above about the plan arm rests on *how the person got there*, never on where they arrived.
- They do not show that parallelism saves time. On this evidence it costs time.
- **They do not measure a person.** Every "person" here is a language model with the card in its
  context, typing at machine speed and never once misremembering the card. `wall_seconds` is the
  span of a run log kept by an agent that also had to fight a sandbox; it is the weakest number in
  this report and cross-arm wall time means very little.
- They do not show anything about a task bigger than this one, an unattended run, or a person who
  has to remember what they wanted.
- **They do not show that the held-out suite would catch a lazy parser.** `quality.py`'s own
  docstring and `PROTOCOL.md` both claim "a parser that passes `accept.py` by pattern-matching the
  sample fails here". The auditor built three readers and measured: a correct one scores 12/12, a
  lazy `el.tag != "summary"` scores **11/12**, and a regex pattern-matched to the literal shape of
  the sample line scores **11/12**. It loses one check of twelve, not the suite. Eleven of the
  twelve properties come free from any `ElementTree` call plus the repository's pre-existing
  `normalize()`, `to_cents(…, "minor")` and negative-price rule — none of which any executor wrote.
  So "12/12 in both arms" is a near-saturated measure and separates much less than it reads.
- **They do not show that the untouched repo is a meaningful floor.** It scores 0/12, but all
  twelve fail with the same note, `exit 2: source is not enabled: xml`. Twelve checks collapse to
  one on a repo where nothing is wired in.
- **They do not show a complete write record in two of the four paragraph-arm runs.** Both Tuesday
  paragraph runs recorded less churn than their own final diff, which is impossible; `tally.py`
  flags it in `notes` and `summarize.py` does not print `notes`. Anything resting on their
  `rework` or `outside (ever)` is a floor.
- **They do not show that the plan arm's `spec_chars` is the person's specification.** Between 69%
  and 73% of it is text the proposing agent wrote, in runs where the person accepted the tree with
  no edits.

## Threats to validity, and the deviations

Everything that went wrong, including what I did wrong.

- **The ceiling is still the ceiling.** Sonnet got everything right in both arms in all nine runs.
  Every difference reported is below that ceiling, and a task designed to break it did not.
- **n = 2 per cell.** Every median in the style table is the average of two numbers. `spec/act`
  1250.9 against 1149.25 is two runs against two runs.
- **The tool's author designed the test**, again: the task, the card, the intent globs, the hidden
  acceptance, the held-out properties and the metric definitions. "Outside intent" still equals
  "outside scope", which the hook enforces, so the plan arm still cannot lose that column.
- **I changed the stand-in brief between repetition 1 and repetition 2.** Twice. Repetition 1 showed
  that a Tuesday stand-in told to write under thirty words wrote 172, and that stand-ins were
  logging `as_me graphene run --with claude -p …` instead of the line they typed, which understates
  their own `person_chars` and flatters the plan arm. Both lines went into every brief from
  repetition 2 on, and both arms of a repetition always had the same brief — but repetition 1 and
  repetition 2 are not quite the same instrument, which is why every run is printed above and not
  only the medians. The `standin.py` commit says the same thing.
